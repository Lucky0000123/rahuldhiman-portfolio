/01

Quarry · UAE · 2022–2024

Greenfield FMS

From bare ground to live dispatch.

60,000 → 90,000tonnes per day
Read engineering dossier ↗

Challenge

The quarry had no fleet management system. Dispatch needed a reliable network, useful operating data and a team ready to use both.

My role

As the site’s first FMS engineer, I led the build from tower survey and network design through commissioning and shift-by-shift optimisation. I assisted Wenco engineers with onboard hardware installation.

Solution

Commissioned solar-powered Rajant mesh coverage, completed Wenco Dynamic Dispatch training and tuned loading, queue and dumping rules. SQL and Power BI put fleet performance in front of supervisors during the shift.

Result & scope

Daily production rose from 60,000 to 90,000 tonnes during this period. Dispatch optimisation, payload monitoring and operator coaching supported the improvement; FMS alone is not claimed as its sole cause. This is a quarry production result, not a claim about the nickel operation.

Wenco FMS / Rajant mesh / GNSS / Power BI

/02

Nickel operation · Indonesia · 2024–present

Mine control & safety

A faster response. A safer haul cycle.

15–20 → 2–3minutes to respond to dispatch
Read engineering dossier ↗

Challenge

Manual dispatch and fragmented visibility made it harder to respond to changing conditions and intervene in unsafe driving.

My role

I own the integrated dispatch and safety platform in an operation with more than 1,000 machines. That is the operation’s scope, not a claim that every machine has been deployed on the platform.

Solution

Established 24/7 mine control rooms, coordinated telemetry and onboard systems, and extended communications with IT and OT teams. Configured zone-based speed enforcement, driver monitoring and collision warnings, backed by operator training.

Result & scope

Dispatch response fell from 15–20 to 2–3 minutes, with around 40% fewer speeding violations and 95%+ platform uptime. More than 3,000 operators trained. AHS work is readiness: networks, workflows and data—not an autonomous production fleet.

Dispatch / Geofencing / Driver monitoring / Radio / AHS readiness

/03

Quarry & processing · UAE · 2016–2024

Crusher control & automation

Connect the quarry to the crushing circuit.

Field → control roomremote circuit visibility and operation
Read engineering dossier ↗

Challenge

Crushing performance depended on coordinated feed, reliable controls and clear visibility. Operators also needed distance from high-noise, high-dust positions.

My role

I led automation delivery and production improvement with electrical, maintenance and specialist teams. In 2024, I designed, established and commissioned the crusher control room.

Solution

Combined PLC/SCADA control, remote camera visibility and automatic feed-rate logic. Worked with the quarry FMS team to synchronise truck movements with crusher demand and supervised throughput against production targets.

Result & scope

Operators could control the full crushing circuit remotely. The work connected production decisions, plant visibility and field response. I led delivery with the team; I do not claim sole authorship of the PLC software.

TRY A SIMPLE THOUGHT EXPERIMENT

What does one minute
of cycle time change?

This is an illustrative arithmetic demo, not my production simulator. All inputs are fictional. It does not use site data or predict real fleet performance.

Tonnes = trucks × payload × (shift hours × 60 ÷ cycle minutes)

Assumes identical trucks, fixed payload and continuous cycles. Excludes queues, availability, utilisation, road limits, loader capacity and partial-trip effects. Fractional cycles are retained.

With the fictional defaults: 14,400 t at 30 min → 14,897 t at 29 min. Theoretical difference: +497 t per shift.
